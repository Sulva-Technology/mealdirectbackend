Part 1

How MealDirect Works

MealDirect is not a general food-delivery app pointed at a campus. It is built around scheduled batch delivery: students order ahead for a fixed meal time, the vendor cooks one batch, and one rider carries the whole batch to a zone. That is why the food is cheap, the delivery fee is flat, and the timing is predictable.

The batch model in one picture

How a campus is structured

Before anyone can order, the campus is described in the system as four nested things. Part 6 walks through creating them.

Layer What it is Example
Campus The university itself. Carries timezone, currency, country and the campus fee ceiling. Venite University · Africa/Lagos · NGN
Zone A delivery cluster inside the campus. Batches are grouped per zone; a zone can carry its own delivery fee. North Hostels (NH), Academic Core (AC)
Location The actual drop point a student picks. Typed hostel or department, with optional delivery instructions. Queen Amina Hall · Faculty of Law
Delivery slot A promised meal time plus a cut-off measured in minutes before it. Orders after the cut-off roll to the next slot. Lunch · delivers 1:00 pm · cut-off 90 min → orders close 11:30 am
Why the cut-off matters more than anything else The cut-off is the single number that decides whether a campus runs smoothly. Too short and vendors cannot cook in time; too long and students feel the app is inflexible. Start at 90 minutes for lunch, review after the pilot week.

The four order states

Everyone — student, vendor, rider, admin — sees the same four states. There are no hidden intermediate steps and no "accepted" or "preparing" taps.

Who does what

Student

Orders before the cut-off, pays by wallet or card, tracks the order, reviews after delivery.

Vendor

Keeps the menu and daily availability honest, cooks the batch, taps Ready once.

Rider

Accepts the batch assignment, confirms whether the food was ready at pickup, delivers.

Campus admin

Runs the campus: zones, slots, vendors, riders, escalations, refunds, daily settlements.

Finance admin

Verifies payments, approves settlements, releases payouts, handles reconciliation.

Support admin

Read-only across the campus. Answers questions and hands real problems to ops or finance.

Two delivery modes, two fulfilment shapes

Mode How it runs When to use it
MealDirect rider
meal_direct_rider		 Batch is assigned to a campus rider who picks up from the vendor and delivers to the zone. Default. Needed once more than one vendor serves a slot.
Vendor delivery
vendor_delivery		 The vendor delivers its own batch and earns the delivery share itself. Very early pilots, or a vendor that already has a delivery person.

Standard delivery vs point delivery

Standard — ₦300

Food comes to the location's normal drop point: the hall entrance, the departmental lobby, wherever the campus agreed. Fastest for the rider, cheapest for the student.

Point delivery — ₦400

The student asks for it to be brought to a specific spot — hostel room, classroom, office — and labels it (e.g. "Block C, Room 214"). The extra ₦100 covers the extra walking; ₦50 of it goes to whoever carries it.

Part 2 · Hand this to students

For Students & Staff

Order lunch between lectures, pay from a wallet you top up by bank transfer, and get it at the time it was promised.

Getting started

  1. Open the app Go to (customer app link) and sign up with your e-mail or phone number. Verify the code you receive.
  2. Enter a referral code, if you have one Referral codes only work before you finish onboarding. Once your profile is complete the window closes permanently — so enter it on the signup screen, not later.
  3. Pick your campus and usual location Choose your hostel or department. This decides which zone you are in and which batch your orders join.
  4. Turn on notifications This is how you find out the food is ready and the rider is on the way. Without it you will be refreshing the app instead.

Placing an order

  1. Pick the meal time first, then the food Choose the service date and delivery slot (e.g. Lunch, delivers 1:00 pm). The app only shows vendors and items actually available for that slot.
  2. Build the plate Set quantities, choose a soup where the item requires one, and add anything the item allows — spoons of rice, protein, drinks, extra sides.
  3. Choose where it lands Standard delivery to your location's drop point, or point delivery to an exact spot for ₦100 more. If it is a hostel, add your room number.
  4. Add a note if it matters Allergies, "no pepper", "call when you're outside". Keep it short — riders read it on a phone in motion.
  5. Apply a promo code Enter it before checkout; the app shows the discount in the total before you pay.
  6. Check the total, then pay Pay from your wallet balance or by card through Paystack. The order is only real once it is paid.
The cut-off is hard Every slot closes a set time before delivery (typically 90 minutes for lunch). After the cut-off, that slot disappears and you order for the next one. An order left unpaid past the cut-off expires automatically — no food, no charge.

What you actually pay

Line Default What it is
Food subtotal varies What the vendor charges for the items you picked.
Delivery fee ₦300 Flat per order. Shared between the rider, the vendor and the platform.
Point-delivery surcharge +₦100 Only if you ask for delivery to a specific room or spot.
Service fee ₦200 Platform fee. Configurable per environment, capped per campus.
Discount − varies Promo code, if applied and valid.
Large-order surcharge 1.5% + ₦100 Only on totals above ₦2,490. Big orders are allowed — you just have to tick the box accepting the surcharge.
Ordering for a group Group orders are welcome. Anything over ₦2,490 asks you to accept the large-order surcharge — that is a confirmation, not a rejection. Tick it and the order goes through.

The wallet

Every student gets a MealDirect wallet with a dedicated bank account number of their own, provided through Paystack. Transfer money to that account and your balance goes up — no card, no re-entering details at every checkout.

Top up

Open Wallet → your account number is on screen. Transfer from any Nigerian bank app. Balance updates when the transfer lands.

Pay

At checkout choose Wallet. If the balance is short, the app tells you exactly how much is missing.

Refunds

Refunds land back in your wallet balance, ready for the next order. Every movement is listed in the wallet ledger.

The ledger records five kinds of movement: topup, order_payment, order_refund, adjustment (an admin correction) and reversal. If a number ever looks wrong, the ledger is the proof — screenshot it when you contact support.

Tracking, chat and delivery

  • paid — the vendor has your order in their worklist.
  • ready — the food is cooked and packed, waiting for the rider.
  • out_for_delivery — the rider has your batch and is moving.
  • delivered — handed over. Reviews open now.

Each batch has a group chat with the other people on the same run, the vendor and the rider. Customers appear under pseudonyms — nobody sees your name or number. Use it for "we're at the Law car park" type coordination, not complaints.

The rider may confirm delivery with a hand-off code you show them. Do not share the code before you have the food in your hand.

Reviews, problems and refunds

  • Review — once an order is delivered you can rate the vendor and leave a comment. This is what keeps campus food quality honest.
  • Report a problem — wrong item, missing item, cold food, never arrived: raise an issue on the order. It goes to campus admins as an escalation, and they can attach a refund to it.
  • Refunds — approved refunds go to your wallet balance. Where a payment must be reversed to the original card instead, admins handle it from the payments console.
Cancelling You can cancel while an order is still paid. Once the vendor has marked it ready the food exists and has been paid for — from that point it is an escalation, not a cancellation.
Part 3 · Hand this to vendors

For Vendors

You cook one batch per slot for a known number of plates, tap Ready once, and get paid on a daily settlement. Guessing demand is the platform's job, not yours.

Onboarding — the once-only setup

  1. Create your vendor account Sign in at (vendor portal) and complete onboarding. A campus admin then approves the vendor before it can appear to students.
  2. Complete the business profile Name, description, contact phone, logo. This is what students see first — upload a real photo of your food, not a stock image.
  3. Add your payout account Bank and account number. The system verifies the account name with the bank before it is usable, and an admin reviews it. No verified payout account means no money can leave the platform to you.
  4. Build the menu Categories first, then items. For each item set price, unit type, the maximum quantity a single order may take, and whether it requires a soup choice.
  5. Add your soup options Any item flagged as requiring soup makes the student pick from this list at checkout — egusi, efo, ogbono, and so on. Keep it to what you genuinely cook every day.
  6. Set item schedules Say which slots and days each item is available. Jollof at lunch only, swallow in the evening — set it once and stop re-answering the question daily.
Unit types decide two things A unit type controls whether a takeaway pack fee applies and whether the item is counted in spoons. Get these right at onboarding — they change what students see and what you are paid for packaging.

The service day

  1. Morning: set availability Before the cut-off, set how much of each item you can actually produce. This is the single most important daily action — availability is what students can order.
  2. Cut-off: the batch closes Orders stop. Your batch list shows the exact number of plates per item. Cook to that number.
  3. Cook, pack, then tap Ready One tap moves the order from paid to ready. There is no accept step and no preparing step — that was removed so you tap once per order, not three times.
  4. Hand over to the rider The rider confirms at pickup whether the food was actually ready. That confirmation decides your on-time bonus, so pack before the rider arrives, not while they wait.
  5. Evening: check the settlement Your daily settlement lists food revenue, your delivery share and any adjustments.

What you earn

Component Amount Condition
Food revenue your prices Less platform commission, as agreed at onboarding.
Delivery-fee share ₦50 / order Paid when the food was ready at least 10 minutes before the slot time, confirmed by the rider at pickup.
Delivery-fee share (late) ₦0 Food not ready in time. The forfeited ₦50 goes to the platform.
Self-delivery rider share On vendor_delivery batches you earn the rider's delivery share too.
The ₦50 is decided at pickup, not at delivery The rider taps "was the food ready?" when they collect the batch. Being 9 minutes early is a forfeit; being 15 minutes early is a bonus. Aim to be packed 20 minutes before the slot and this line never costs you anything.

Getting paid

  1. Settlement generated — a daily settlement is produced from your delivered orders.
  2. Admin approves — a finance or campus admin reviews and approves it.
  3. Payout sent — an automated Paystack transfer to your verified account, or a manual payment marked in the console if automated payouts are off.
  4. Reconciled — the transfer status is tracked until the bank confirms; failed transfers are retried and surfaced to admins.

Vendor rules that protect you

  • You are never asked to cook on speculation — every plate in a closed batch is already paid for.
  • An order you genuinely cannot fulfil can be cancelled while it is still paid; the student is refunded.
  • Only your own vendor's orders, menu and settlements are visible to you. Other vendors cannot see your numbers, and you cannot see theirs.
  • Reviews are tied to delivered orders only. Nobody can review food they never received.
Vendor staff accounts A vendor can have several user accounts — owner, kitchen lead, cashier. Ask the campus admin to invite them. Everyone sees the same vendor worklist, so agree who taps Ready before service starts.
Part 4 · Hand this to riders

For Riders

One trip, many orders, one zone. Your earnings depend on one thing you control — arriving on time — and one thing you report honestly: whether the food was ready.

Onboarding

  1. Sign up as a rider At (rider portal), complete rider onboarding with your name, phone and vehicle type.
  2. Get verified A campus admin verifies your identity and campus eligibility. Until then you receive no assignments.
  3. Add your payout account Bank and account number, verified against the bank's records and reviewed by an admin. Earnings cannot be paid out without it.
  4. Set yourself available Assignments only reach riders who are marked available. Turn it off when you are in class.

A delivery run, step by step

  1. Assignment arrives You get a batch: one vendor, one slot, one zone, N orders. Accept or decline promptly — declining fast lets ops reassign before the food gets cold. Declining with a reason is not held against you; silence is.
  2. Go to the vendor and mark picked up Count the packs against the batch list before you leave. Wrong counts found at the hostel are your problem; wrong counts found at the kitchen are the vendor's.
  3. Answer the readiness question honestly When you start the run you confirm whether the food was ready. This decides the vendor's ₦50 bonus. It is not a complaint mechanism and it is not optional — answer what actually happened.
  4. Ride out The batch moves to out_for_delivery and every student in it is notified.
  5. Deliver each order Mark each one delivered as you hand it over, or scan/enter the student's hand-off code. Do not mark a whole batch delivered from the car park.
  6. Raise an issue if something is wrong Nobody at the door, wrong address, damaged pack — report it on the order instead of improvising. It becomes an escalation an admin can resolve.

What you earn per order

Situation You earn Note
Delivered on time ₦100 Measured against the slot's promised delivery time.
Delivered late ₦50 The forfeited ₦50 goes to the platform, not to the vendor.
Point delivery +₦50 Paid on any successful point delivery — this one is not timing-gated.

So a full run of on-time point deliveries pays ₦150 per order, and a late standard delivery pays ₦50. Across a 20-order batch that is the difference between ₦3,000 and ₦1,000 — timing is the whole job.

The fastest way to protect your earnings Get to the vendor before the slot time, not at it. If the vendor is not packed, report it at pickup — that protects you when the delivery lands late.

Earnings and payouts

  • Earnings — running totals per day and per settlement period, in the rider portal.
  • Settlements — generated daily from delivered orders, then approved by an admin.
  • Payout history — every transfer to your bank, with its status.
Never accept cash for a MealDirect order Every order in the app is already paid. A student asking to "pay you directly" is either confused or trying it on — point them at the app and report it to campus support.
Part 5 · Hand this to the campus team

For Campus Admins & Ops

The admin console is where a campus is configured, watched and unblocked. This part covers who can do what, the daily routine, and how money leaves the platform.

Admin roles and what each can do

Admin rights come from an explicit membership record — never from a user editing their own profile. Everyone can read; the columns below add write powers.

Role Can do Give it to
super_admin Everything, across every campus, including managing other admins. 1–2 people at the platform company. Not the campus.
campus_admin Everything for their campus: vendors, riders, users, refunds, wallets, settlements, payouts, reconciliation, payment verification. The campus lead who owns the launch.
finance_admin Payments, refunds, wallets, settlements, payouts, reconciliation. No vendor/rider management. Whoever reconciles money daily.
operations_admin Vendors and riders. No money powers at all. The person recruiting and managing kitchens and riders.
support_admin Read-only. Sees orders, batches and escalations to answer questions. Student support volunteers and the front desk.
readonly_admin Read-only. Reporting and oversight. University stakeholders who want visibility, not control.
Separate money from operations Do not give the person who recruits vendors the power to approve settlements to those same vendors. operations_admin and finance_admin exist precisely so one person cannot both create a payee and pay them.

The daily routine

Before the cut-off
  • Check every serving vendor has set availability.
  • Check enough riders are marked available per zone.
  • Watch order volume per batch; flag a batch that is unusually large.
At the cut-off
  • Batches close. Confirm each has an assigned fulfiller.
  • Assign or reassign riders where a batch is unassigned or declined.
  • Switch a batch to vendor delivery if no rider is available.
During service
  • Watch orders still paid close to the slot time — those are late kitchens.
  • Answer batch chat where students are confused.
  • Pick up escalations as they arrive; assign an owner to each.
After service
  • Resolve open escalations, attaching refunds where justified.
  • Clear the payment problem queues (mismatches, unverified payments).
  • Preview, generate and approve settlements.

Handling problems

Situation What to do
Student paid but order shows unpaid Reconcile the payment against Paystack from the payments console. If the money is genuinely there and the webhook was lost, an admin with the right permission can force the payment to paid — this is a deliberate, audited override, not a routine fix.
Rider declined and nobody picked up the batch Reassign the rider, or assign the batch to vendor delivery.
Vendor cannot fulfil after cut-off Cancel the affected orders (refunds follow) and notify the batch chat. Log it against the vendor's performance.
Food never arrived Open the escalation, request evidence, resolve with a refund to the student's wallet.
Order stuck in a state after the fact Use the administrative status transition to close it out. Every transition is audit-logged with the actor.
Wallet balance disputed Open the wallet ledger, find the entry, and post an adjustment if it is genuinely wrong. Freeze the wallet if fraud is suspected.

Money, end to end

Settlement and payout controls

  1. Preview — see what a settlement run would produce before committing.
  2. Generate — create the settlement records for the period.
  3. Approve — a finance-permission action. Only approved settlements can be paid.
  4. Pay — automated Paystack transfer to the verified payout account, or mark-paid for a manual bank transfer.
  5. Reconcile — transfer status is tracked and retried; failures land in a queue for an admin.
  6. Adjust — corrections are posted as explicit adjustments against the settlement, never by editing history.
Everything sensitive is audited Refunds, force-paid overrides, wallet adjustments, status transitions, vendor and rider state changes and payout approvals all write an audit entry naming the admin who did it. Tell your team this on day one — it changes behaviour for the better.

Growth levers you control

Promotions

Create discount codes, watch redemption, deactivate when the budget is spent.

Referrals

Every student has a code. Referral analytics show who is actually bringing people in.

Vendor performance

Per-vendor stats on volume, readiness and reviews — the basis for keeping or dropping a kitchen.

Part 6 · The operator playbook

Launching a New University

Six phases, roughly six weeks. The sequence matters: a campus that recruits vendors before agreeing meal times ends up rebuilding its menus, and a campus that goes wide before a pilot week burns its first impression on students who only try once.

Phase 0 — Discovery and agreement

Before touching the console, leave discovery with these answers written down.

Question Why it decides something Answer
Who owns this campus internally? One named campus admin. Launches without an owner stall in week two.
Which hostels and faculties are in scope? Becomes your zones and locations. Start narrow — two zones beats eight.
When do students actually eat? Becomes your delivery slots. Ask students, not the administration.
Which kitchens already feed this campus? Your vendor shortlist. Existing demand beats a new kitchen every time.
Are bikes allowed on campus, and where? Decides rider mode, zone shapes and realistic delivery windows.
Is university permission needed to operate? Security stopping riders at the gate on day one is a launch-ending problem.

Phase 1 — Configure the campus

  1. Create the campus Name, slug, timezone (Africa/Lagos), currency NGN, country NG. Leave it inactive until the pilot.
  2. Create zones A short code and display order each. Two to four zones for a launch; add more when volume justifies a second rider run.
  3. Create locations Each hostel and department, typed correctly, with delivery instructions that a rider who has never been there can follow ("gate 2, ask for the porter's desk").
  4. Create delivery slots Name, delivery time and cut-off minutes. Start with one or two slots. One slot done well beats four half-served.
  5. Confirm fees and settings Delivery base, point surcharge, rider and vendor shares, service fee, large-order threshold. Check the live values rather than assuming the defaults.
Launch shape that works 1 slot × 2 zones × 3 vendors × 2 riders. Everything else is a week-four decision.

Phase 2 — Vendors

  1. Shortlist — kitchens students already trust, ideally already cooking at volume.
  2. Explain the model honestly — cook to a known number, get paid daily, ₦50 per order for being early, ₦0 for being late.
  3. Onboard and approve — vendor completes onboarding; an admin approves before they are visible.
  4. Verify payout accounts — do this in week two, not the night before launch. Bank name mismatches take days to sort out.
  5. Build menus together — sit with each vendor for the first menu. Prices, unit types, soup options, max quantities, photos.
  6. Rehearse a service day — place a test order, have them set availability, cook it and tap Ready. Do this before students exist.

Phase 3 — Riders and the campus team

  1. Recruit riders — students with bikes are usually the best first riders; they know the halls.
  2. Onboard, verify, add payout accounts — no verified account means no earnings can be paid.
  3. Walk the zones — physically walk each drop point with the riders before launch day.
  4. Set up admins — one campus admin, one finance admin, one or two operations admins, support as read-only.
  5. Agree the on-call rota — who answers the phone during each slot, in writing.

Phase 4 — Pilot week

One slot, invited students only, capped volume. The pilot is not a demo — it is where you find the wrong cut-off, the hostel with no signage and the vendor who cooks in 90 minutes, not 60.

Watch Healthy If it is worse
Orders ready before the slot time > 90% Lengthen the cut-off or reduce the vendor's availability numbers.
Delivered on time > 85% Zones are too big or there are too few riders per run.
Escalations per 100 orders < 5 Read them all — the same cause usually repeats.
Wallet payment share rising weekly Students have not found the top-up flow; push it in orientation.
Payment problem queue empty daily Reconcile before it accumulates; a stale queue hides real losses.

Phase 5 — Go-live gates

Do not open to the whole campus until every box below is ticked.

Configuration
  • Campus active, zones and locations complete
  • Delivery slots with agreed cut-offs
  • Fee settings confirmed against the console
Supply
  • ≥ 3 approved vendors with live menus and photos
  • Every vendor payout account verified
  • ≥ 2 verified riders with payout accounts, per zone
People
  • Campus admin named and trained
  • Finance admin able to approve a settlement unaided
  • Support rota published with a phone number students can reach
Proof
  • Pilot week completed with metrics above the thresholds
  • One full settlement generated, approved and paid out
  • One refund issued end to end
  • One escalation resolved end to end

Part 7

Troubleshooting & FAQ

The questions every campus asks in week one, grouped by who asks them.

Students ask

Question Answer
"Why can't I order lunch now?" The cut-off has passed. Order for the next slot — the app only shows slots still open.
"I transferred money, the wallet is empty." Bank transfers can lag. Open the wallet ledger; if nothing appears after a few minutes, contact support with the transfer reference.
"I paid but the order says unpaid." Support reconciles the payment against Paystack. Do not pay twice.
"My order is above ₦2,490 and won't go through." Accept the large-order surcharge (1.5% + ₦100) on the checkout screen. Big orders are allowed.
"Can I change my delivery location after paying?" Not in-app. Ask in the batch chat before the rider leaves; otherwise it is an escalation.
"Where do I claim my referral bonus?" Enter the code during signup, before completing onboarding. After that the window is closed permanently.
"Nobody delivered my food." Report a problem on the order. It becomes an escalation; an approved refund goes to your wallet.

Vendors ask

Question Answer
"No orders came in today." Availability was probably not set before the cut-off. Nothing available means nothing orderable.
"An item isn't showing to students." Check three things in order: item active, schedule covers this slot and day, availability set for today.
"I didn't get the ₦50 bonus." The rider confirmed the food was not ready at pickup, or it was ready less than 10 minutes before the slot time.
"Where is my money?" Settlements are daily and must be approved before payout. Check settlement status first, then payout status.
"I need to cancel an order." Possible while it is still paid. Once marked ready, raise it with the campus admin.
"Can my cashier have their own login?" Yes — ask the campus admin to invite a vendor user. Agree who taps Ready.

Riders ask

Question Answer
"I'm not getting assignments." Check: verified by an admin, marked available, and a batch exists in your zone for that slot.
"The vendor wasn't ready — am I penalised?" No. Answer the readiness question honestly at pickup; that is exactly what it protects against.
"The student isn't answering." Message the batch chat, wait the agreed grace period, then raise an issue on the order. Do not leave food unattended.
"I was paid ₦50 instead of ₦100." That order was delivered after the slot's promised time.
"My payout failed." Usually an unverified or mismatched bank account. Re-verify in the rider portal and tell the admin.

Admins ask

Question Answer
"A batch has no rider and the slot is close." Reassign a rider, or switch the batch to vendor delivery. Both are console actions.
"A payment is stuck in the problem queue." Reconcile against Paystack first. Only use force-paid when the money is provably received — it is audited.
"Should a refund go to the wallet or the card?" Wallet by default — instant and keeps the student on the platform. Reverse to the card only when the student is leaving or explicitly asks.
"A vendor keeps missing readiness." Vendor performance shows the pattern. Coach first, reduce their availability second, suspend third.
"Someone needs admin access." Grant the narrowest role that does the job. Never super_admin for a campus person.
"Can we change the delivery fee for one zone?" Yes — zones carry their own delivery fee. The rider and vendor shares stay fixed and the platform absorbs the difference.

Point deliveryDelivery to a specific spot (room, class, office) for an extra ₦100.
Dedicated accountThe bank account number issued to each student's wallet for top-ups by transfer.
LedgerThe itemised record of every wallet movement — the source of truth in a dispute.
SettlementThe daily statement of what a vendor or rider is owed, before payout.
PayoutThe actual bank transfer of an approved settlement.
EscalationA logged problem on an order, assigned to an admin, resolvable with a refund.
ForfeitA rider or vendor share not earned because of timing. Forfeits always go to the platform.
